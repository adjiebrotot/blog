# Editorial safeguards

Authoritative for every language. These safeguards override a style preference or a
brevity target. Publication mechanics remain in CLAUDE.md and README.md. Indonesian
language decisions remain in bahasa-indonesia, with explicit author preference approvals.

## Evidence classes

The brief gives each claim or figure an ID, class, provenance and material conditions:

- **Sourced fact:** checked source passage or table, publisher, URL, date, unit, population
  and definition. A citation alone does not establish that the source supports the claim.
- **Model output:** tool or calculation, inputs, horizon, units, nominal or real basis,
  uncertainty or success probability where applicable, and reproducible result.
- **Assumption:** explicitly identified scenario input, not a verified population fact.
- **Personal observation:** supplied or confirmed author material and relevant context/date.
- **Interpretation:** the reasoning from evidence and its limits, not an observation or fact.

These are brief annotations, not mandatory labels before every paragraph in the article.
Use natural prose to keep distinctions visible. Unverified claims do not reach publication.

## Personal provenance

Only Adjie can confirm autobiography. Never invent or embellish memories, dialogue,
feelings, motives, actions, sensory details, observations or personal financial results
for warmth, credibility or a voice requirement. An earlier AI-generated post is not proof
that its personal claims happened. Mark uncertain personal material for confirmation.
If unanswered, omit it or use a non-personal explanation; never silently confirm it.
Exact remembered dialogue must be confirmed as a quotation, otherwise use a faithful
paraphrase of supplied material. Do not request unnecessary sensitive personal information.

## Conditions travel with the claim

Do not turn a model result into a general fact, an assumption into a forecast, or a
cross-sectional comparison into evidence of a trend or cause. Preserve conditions that
change a decision in the title/summary, section answer, verdict and recommendation wherever
that claim is made. If the headline result depends on a low success probability, a chosen
investment return, an eligibility rule or a comparison horizon, keep that condition beside
it. A footnote or generic disclaimer is not a substitute. A summary may omit supporting
figures, but not a decision-changing condition. If space is tight, narrow the claim.

## Minimum sufficient evidence

Keep the evidence the argument needs: baseline, comparator, units, date, definition and
material assumptions or qualifications. There is no one-number-per-claim quota. Comparisons
may require several numbers. Cut incidental dimensions, dates, programme names and funding
details only when they do not support the argument. Do not cut proof for a fixed reduction
percentage or a universal word limit.

## Purposeful repetition and section independence

Avoid redundant explanation. Repeat a fact only when needed for a summary, comparison or
independently understandable section. Prose may interpret a table rather than recite it.
Keep the blog same-section pointer rule: move the figure to where it is discussed or state
the needed fact locally. Repetition for that purpose is allowed; a second full explanation
is not required. Sections can still form a progressive argument. Clear questions, statements
and short labels are equally valid headings; no question-heading or answer-first quota.

## Substantive editorial review

Use a fresh editor for a new post or rewrite, separate from writers and language evaluators.
It reads the full post, relevant evidence ledger and approved primary intention. Check:

1. Does the evidence support each factual and interpretive claim, not just its citation?
2. Are comparisons compatible in definitions, populations, time, units and uncertainty?
3. Does a causal or trend conclusion require evidence the post has not supplied?
4. Do summaries and recommendations preserve decision-changing qualifications?
5. Is the strongest relevant objection answered substantively rather than ceremonially?
6. Which paragraphs repeat explanation, drift from the intention, or add unneeded details?
7. Are personal claims confirmed and all material uncertainties resolved?
8. Do rhetorical devices and announcements repeat mechanically within or across posts?

Report exact passage/line, problem, evidence, and smallest proposed fix. Classify:
**must fix** for unsupported claims, lost conditions or unconfirmed personal assertions;
**editorial suggestion** for acceptable stylistic alternatives. Do not rewrite the entire
post into the editor voice or invent stronger evidence. Unresolved substantive issues go
back for research or an author decision; never force publication by repeated stylistic edits.
Mechanical checks remain necessary but do not prove good writing or factual correctness.
